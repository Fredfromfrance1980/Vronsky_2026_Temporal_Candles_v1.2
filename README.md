## English

### What it is

Tests of general relativity (GR) are published in heterogeneous parameters, and anomalies such as the dynamics of disc galaxies are usually stated relative to a model. There is no single picture showing where gravity has been calibrated, where it has not, and where a given anomaly sits. This paper builds one.

By analogy with the standard candles of the distance ladder, a **temporal candle** is a system whose mass is known independently of the effect being measured, and for which the ratio

ℛ = observed effect / GR prediction

is published with an uncertainty. Each candle is placed in dimensionless coordinates built from ratios of characteristic times (see the companion paper *Temporal fingerprints*):

| Symbol | Definition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| X | GM/(Rc²) | compactness (gravitational time / light-crossing time) |
| Y | M_P/M | Planck time / gravitational time |
| g/a_P | X²Y | Newtonian acceleration in Planck units |

Five rules make a candle usable: independent mass (C1), independent coordinates (C2), domain of validity (C3), clock state (C4), declared status, precision or indicative (C5). The workflow has four steps: calibrate, overlay, read, forecast.

**This is a methods paper.** All analyses are carried out within classical general relativity (Newtonian limit for wide binaries). MOND with the external-field effect appears only as the alternative that the wide-binary test is designed to discriminate.

### Main results

1. **Calibration map.** Eight tests (six precision, two indicative), from Gravity Probe A to GW150914, give ℛ = 1 over nine decades of compactness (six with the precision candles alone), but only at high acceleration (−52.1 ≤ log g/a_P ≤ −49.1 for the precision candles).
2. **Overlay: disc galaxies.** 2,511 rotation-curve points from 155 SPARC galaxies share the candles' compactness but lie about eight decades lower in acceleration. Their departure from ℛ = 1 is organised by acceleration
